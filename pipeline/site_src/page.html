      How it works

      Strength is net EPA per play: how many points each snap adds on offense, minus what the defense allows, adjusted for opponents and shown as points per game better or worse than an average team. It beat both win-loss record and point differential at predicting later games in 2022–2025 tests. It does not beat the Vegas line.

      Game picks start from strength and home field, then adjust for three things: a quarterback change (the new starter's track record against the quarterback play already in the rating), starters ruled out on the injury report (weighted by how much they play), and rest days. Each was added only after it made the picks more accurate on seasons it wasn't tuned on. Vegas lines are never an input; they're only the yardstick.

      Preseason starts each team at half of last season's strength. That starting point fades each week and is gone by midseason, after which only this season's play counts.

      Luck is wins minus the wins a team with its strength would usually have. Big gaps tend to close: one-score records, turnover margin and opponents' kicking barely repeat.

      Verdicts: Contender is one of the very strongest teams. Solid is good play with a record to match. Good but lucky is a good team winning more than its play earns. Better than their record is a good team losing more than it deserves. Fluff is a winning record built on weak play or luck. Verdicts start once a few weeks of games are in.
